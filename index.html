Strip

Your windows, on an endless strip.

Strip puts your windows side by side on a horizontal strip that runs past the edges of your screen. Open another app and the strip simply gets longer. Nothing shrinks, nothing overlaps, and every window keeps the width you gave it.

What it does

Keyboard first. ⌥H / ⌥L to move along the strip, ⌥⇧H / ⌥⇧L to move a window, ⌥R to cycle ⅓ · ½ · ⅔, ⌥F full width, ⌥C center. Every shortcut can be rebound.
Stacks and tabs. ⌥[ / ⌥] stack a window into its neighbour's column, ⌥J / ⌥K move within it, ⌥T switches a stack to tabs.
Every display, every Space. Each display gets its own strip, and each Space keeps its own layout.
Calm. Windows glide into place, a soft border shows focus, and Strip remembers each app's favourite width.
Scriptable. strip:// links, a strip terminal command and Raycast scripts.

Common questions

Why does Strip need Accessibility access?
macOS only lets apps move other apps' windows through the Accessibility permission. Strip uses it for exactly that and never reads what's inside your windows.

Why isn't Strip on the Mac App Store?
App Store apps must run in Apple's sandbox, which blocks the window-moving permission every window manager needs. Strip is signed with Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple instead.

I bought Pro. How do I unlock another Mac?
